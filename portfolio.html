Engineering Mindset
How I think about
systems.

The mental models that guide my decisions when building backend systems.

01
Observability before optimization
You can't optimize what you can't measure. Before touching query plans or caching layers, I instrument the system — structured logs, error rates, slow query alerts. Every change is validated with data, not intuition.
02
Design for the failure case
Happy paths are easy. I think about what happens when the database is slow, the Slack webhook retries three times, or two users hit the same record simultaneously. Idempotency, optimistic locking, and circuit breakers are design decisions, not afterthoughts.
03
Boring tech > clever tech
PostgreSQL over exotic DBs. REST over GraphQL when it's simpler. The right tool is the one your team understands, can debug at 2AM, and scales without drama. I reach for proven primitives and add complexity only when genuinely needed.
04
Interfaces, not implementations
Good backend design means strong contracts between services. I think in API boundaries, data shapes, and event contracts first — then fill in the implementation. Loose coupling at the seams, strict typing within boundaries.
